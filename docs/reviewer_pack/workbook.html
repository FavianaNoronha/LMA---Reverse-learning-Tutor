SETU · native reviewer workbook

For a reviewer who speaks both Telugu and Hindi. You make the tutor's language right; your answers decide which sentences learners will see. Thank you.

What SETU is. A tutor where Hindi speakers learn Telugu (and later Telugu speakers learn Hindi) by correcting an AI classmate, Raju. Raju makes the mistakes a Hindi speaker typically makes in Telugu; the learner fixes them. Every mistake and every correct sentence comes from a small rule book. Today the rule book was written by us with an AI and has not been checked by a native speaker. That is your job.

Total time: about 4–5 hours, in any order, over a few sittings. Tasks 1 and 3 matter most. Please don't use an AI tool to answer; we need your own judgement. If you are unsure, write “unsure” and why: that is a useful answer.

Your tasks

≈ 2 h · most importantTask 1 · Check the 15 Hindi→Telugu rules (30 sentences)
File: 1_review_hindi_to_telugu_rules.csv. For every row answer Q1–Q6 (guide below). Most important: is Raju's sentence a mistake Hindi speakers really make, and is the “correct” Telugu natural and standard?
≈ 1 h · most importantTask 3 · Mark 145 learner answers
File: 3_mark_learner_answers_BLIND.csv. For each answer write correct, wrong or unsure for the English meaning given. Judge as a teacher would: would you accept it in class? Spelling slips count as wrong; a different but correct way of saying it counts as correct. The tutor's own verdicts are hidden on purpose; we compare them with yours afterwards.
≈ 1.5 hTask 2 · Check the 10 Telugu→Hindi draft rules (20 sentences)
File: 2_review_telugu_to_hindi_rules.csv. Same questions, the other way round: mistakes Telugu speakers make in Hindi (gender, ने, oblique मेरे, joined postpositions, missing है, relative clauses). Is each a real Telugu-speaker mistake? Is the Hindi standard? Please also suggest rules we missed in Task 5.
≈ 20 minTask 4 · Check the Hindi-letter reading aid
File: 4_check_script_conversion.csv. The tutor shows every Telugu sentence in Hindi letters (Devanagari) so Hindi speakers can read it. For each row: would a Hindi reader say it correctly from these letters? If not, write a better spelling. Watch short ఎ/ఒ (shown as ऎ/ऒ), ళ (shown as ळ) and words ending in ం.
≈ 1 h · optionalTask 5 · Add spoken forms and missing rules
File: 5_new_rules_and_spoken_forms.csv. Hyderabad Telugu as spoken often differs from textbook Telugu (e.g. వస్తున్నా beside వస్తున్నాను). Write spoken forms learners will hear, and mistakes you have heard Hindi speakers make in Telugu (or Telugu speakers in Hindi) that are not in our list.

How to answer Q1–Q6

QuestionWhat to write
Q1 Is Raju's mistake realistic?often / sometimes / rarely / never. Would a Hindi speaker learning Telugu really say this? “never” means we should drop or change the rule.
Q2 Exactly one mistake?yes, or no and what the other mistake is. Raju's sentence must be correct except for the one planted mistake.
Q3 Is the correct sentence natural?yes / formal (correct but bookish) / no, and write the better sentence in Q4.
Q4 Other correct formsEvery other way a learner could correctly say it, including spoken forms. Separate with |. The tutor accepts only what is listed, so this column matters.
Q5 Is the explanation right?yes / partly / no, with a corrected explanation. This text is shown to learners as the hint.
Q6 LevelA1 (first weeks: survival phrases), A2 (simple everyday sentences), B1 (connected speech). Rough is fine; it orders the lessons.
Example of a filled row · HI-TE-002-a · “This is a book.” · Raju: ఇది పుస్తకం ఉంది · correct: ఇది పుస్తకం → Q1 often · Q2 yes · Q3 yes · Q4 ఇది ఒక పుస్తకం · Q5 yes · Q6 A1 · notes Hindi speakers add ఉంది because of है.

Returning your work

  1. Open the CSV files in Google Sheets or Excel (they open directly; keep the column order and don't rename columns).
  2. Fill the empty columns. Leave a cell empty if you skip it.
  3. Send the files back (or share the Google Sheet). We update the rule book, mark each rule native_checked with your initials and the date, and re-run the tests.
  4. With your permission we will thank you by name in the paper's acknowledgements. We do not collect any personal data about you.
